## Intro

This is the NVIDIA generational matchup: Blackwell B200 vs Hopper H200. Both are available at hyperscalers, and the question is simple: is B200 worth the premium? We tested both across 7 models on [InferenceX](https://inferencex.semianalysis.com). Short answer: yes, it is.

## Key Numbers

- B200 wins on throughput in all 7 shared models. No exceptions.
- Average throughput advantage: **246%** (2.5x faster).
- Largest gap: DeepSeek-R1-0528, where B200 is 5.5x faster (13,429.7 vs 2,453.9 tok/s/GPU).
- B200 is also cheaper per Mtok on all 7 models because its throughput outpaces its $/hr premium.

B200 is cheaper per token on every model. The savings range from 46% on Llama-3.3-70B to 75% on DeepSeek-R1-0528. This is a clean generational upgrade: more throughput, lower cost per token, and access to FP4 precision (which H200 doesn't support on most models).

## Why the Gap Is So Large on DeepSeek-R1-0528

The 5.5x gap on DeepSeek-R1-0528 is the largest across all models. Three factors compound: (1) B200 runs FP4 while H200 is limited to FP8, (2) B200 uses dynamo-trt with disaggregated prefill while H200 uses plain trt, and (3) B200 runs at concurrency 1,691 while H200 tops out at 256. On models where both use the same framework and precision (like MiniMax-M2.5 with vllm FP8), the gap narrows to 186%.

## When H200 Still Makes Sense

H200 is cheaper per hour than B200, so if your workload is low-utilization (sporadic requests, not batch), the idle cost matters. H200 also has lower TTFT at low concurrency on some models, like Llama-3.3-70B (766.0ms with vllm vs B200's 509.2ms with vllm at the same concurrency). If you're running a chatbot with low QPS, H200's lower hourly rate and competitive TTFT might make it the right call.
