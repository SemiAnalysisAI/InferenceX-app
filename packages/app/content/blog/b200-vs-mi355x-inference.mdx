## Intro

NVIDIA B200 vs AMD MI355X is the matchup everyone is watching. B200 is NVIDIA's current-gen Blackwell card; MI355X is AMD's answer. Both are the top picks from their respective vendors. We ran them head-to-head across 7 models on [InferenceX](https://inferencex.semianalysis.com).

## Key Numbers

- B200 wins on throughput in 6 of 7 shared models, by 124% on average.
- On DeepSeek-R1-0528, B200 is 3.2x faster: **13,429.7 vs 4,222.8 tok/s/GPU**.
- MI355X wins MiniMax-M2.5 (10,391.0 vs 9,652.0 tok/s/GPU), an 8% edge.
- On Kimi-K2.5, the gap is just 15% and MI355X is cheaper per token ($0.12 vs $0.13/Mtok).

B200 wins on cost in 4 models, MI355X in 2, and they tie on gpt-oss-120b. The models where MI355X wins on cost (Kimi-K2.5, MiniMax-M2.5) are also the ones where the throughput gap is smallest. On models where B200 has a large throughput lead, it also wins on cost because the higher throughput more than offsets its higher $/hr.

## Where MI355X Competes

MiniMax-M2.5 is MI355X's strongest showing: 8% faster than B200 and 33% cheaper per token. Kimi-K2.5 is competitive too, with just a 15% throughput gap and lower cost. Both models use atom or vllm on MI355X, frameworks that seem well-optimized for AMD hardware. On models requiring dynamo-trt or TensorRT-LLM (NVIDIA-only frameworks), MI355X can't compete.

## The DeepSeek-R1 Gap

B200's biggest win is on DeepSeek-R1-0528: 218% faster than MI355X. This is mostly a framework story. B200 runs dynamo-trt with disaggregated prefill; MI355X uses mori-sglang. On this model, disagg prefill is worth 57% throughput on NVIDIA hardware, and MI355X's best framework (mori-sglang) is still maturing. As AMD's serving stack improves, this gap should narrow.

## Methodology
