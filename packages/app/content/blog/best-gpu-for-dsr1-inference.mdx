DeepSeek-R1-0528 is a 671B mixture-of-experts reasoning model. Its size means most GPUs need FP4 quantization just to fit it, making precision and framework choice unusually important. We benchmarked it across 9 GPUs on [InferenceX](https://inferencex.semianalysis.com); for a smaller dense model comparison, see our [gpt-oss-120b benchmarks](/blog/best-gpu-for-gptoss120b-inference).

## Key Findings

- NVIDIA GB300 on FP4 hits **18,131.6 tok/s/GPU**, the fastest config we've tested for DeepSeek-R1-0528.
- NVIDIA B300 on FP4 reaches 15,868.1 tok/s/GPU, 88% of the GB300's throughput with similar cost per token.
- Disaggregated prefill pushes the GB300 from 11,585.2 to 18,131.6 tok/s/GPU (a 57% gain). It matters on this model.
- AMD MI355X on FP4 with mori-sglang gets 4,222.8 tok/s/GPU. That's the best AMD result, but still 77% behind the GB300.
- NVIDIA B200 on FP4 at $0.04/Mtok is the cheapest option we tested while still delivering 13,429.7 tok/s/GPU.

## Which GPU should you pick for DeepSeek-R1-0528?

NVIDIA GB300 on FP4 with dynamo-trt, if you can get the cards. It's 14% faster than the B300 and 35% faster than the B200, with TPOT under 34ms. If you're on AMD, the MI355X on FP4 with mori-sglang gets 4,222.8 tok/s/GPU, though latency is high (71s TTFT at concurrency 2,048). For budget deployments, the B200 on FP4 matches the GB300's $0.04/Mtok cost with 74% of the throughput.

## FP4 vs FP8 on DeepSeek-R1-0528

On the GB300, FP4 roughly doubles throughput vs FP8: 18,131.6 vs 8,980.3 tok/s/GPU. TPOT drops from 52.9ms to 33.6ms too. The gap is similar on the B200, where FP4 hits 13,429.7 vs 5,856.9 tok/s/GPU on FP8. We haven't measured accuracy loss from FP4 quantization on this model, so check your eval suite before committing. On AMD hardware, only MI355X has FP4 support right now.

## dynamo-trt vs sglang vs mori-sglang

Split decision by hardware. dynamo-trt dominates on NVIDIA Blackwell (GB300, B300, GB200, B200), delivering the top throughput across the board. On AMD MI355X, mori-sglang with FP4 disaggregated prefill gets 4,222.8 tok/s/GPU, beating atom's 3,652.0. Plain sglang on MI355X only manages 1,952.2 tok/s/GPU. Framework matters as much as the GPU on this model.

## Does disaggregated prefill help?

Yes, across the board on NVIDIA hardware. On the GB300, disagg pushes throughput from 11,585.2 to 18,131.6 tok/s/GPU (a 57% gain). The B200 sees a similar jump: 10,505.4 to 13,429.7 tok/s/GPU (28% gain). On the GB200, disagg improves throughput from 11,585.2 to 14,952.0 tok/s/GPU. TTFT goes up at high concurrency (the tradeoff for batching prefill separately), but the throughput gain is worth it for batch workloads.

## NVIDIA GB300 vs NVIDIA B300 on DeepSeek-R1-0528

The GB300 wins on throughput (18,131.6 vs 15,868.1 tok/s/GPU), a 14% edge. TPOT is close: 33.6ms vs 38.5ms. TTFT is also similar at high concurrency: 3,358.3ms vs 2,773.4ms. Both run FP4 on dynamo-trt with disaggregated prefill. The B300 actually has slightly lower TTFT, so for latency-sensitive workloads it's competitive. For pure throughput, GB300 is the pick.

## Fastest time to first token

AMD MI325X on FP8 with sglang at concurrency 64: 489.8ms TTFT. That's far faster than the GB300's best TTFT (3,358.3ms), though throughput drops to just 907.6 tok/s/GPU at that concurrency. AMD MI300X is close behind at 661.5ms. If TTFT matters more than throughput (interactive chat, for example), the older AMD cards at low concurrency are the configs to run.

## Cheapest inference per million tokens

NVIDIA B200 on FP4 at $0.04/Mtok (hyperscaler pricing). That's tied with the GB300 ($0.04/Mtok) despite 26% lower throughput, because the B200's $/hr is lower. The GB200 and B300 are also at $0.04/Mtok. AMD MI355X comes in at $0.10/Mtok, 2.4x more expensive per token. If you're optimizing purely for cost, any Blackwell GPU on FP4 with dynamo-trt is the sweet spot.
