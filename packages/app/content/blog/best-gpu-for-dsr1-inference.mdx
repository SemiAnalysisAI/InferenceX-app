DeepSeek-R1-0528 is a 671B mixture-of-experts reasoning model. Loading it without quantization requires a large GPU fleet, so precision and framework choice matter more than usual. We benchmarked it across 9 GPUs on [InferenceX](https://inferencex.semianalysis.com); for a smaller dense model comparison, see our [Llama-3.3-70B benchmarks](/blog/best-gpu-for-llama70b-inference).

## Key Findings

- NVIDIA GB300 on FP4 hits **18,131.6 tok/s/GPU**, the fastest config we've tested for DeepSeek-R1-0528.
- NVIDIA B300 on FP4 comes in at 15,868.1 tok/s/GPU, which is 87.5% of the GB300. Closer than you'd expect for a non-NVLink config.
- FP4 dominates FP8 on the GB300: 18,131.6 vs 8,980.3 tok/s/GPU, a 2x gap. Worth checking your accuracy budget.
- AMD MI355X manages 4,222.8 tok/s/GPU on FP4, the best AMD result but still 23% of the GB300.
- Last-gen H200 still posts 2,453.9 tok/s/GPU on FP8. Not fast, but serviceable for low-concurrency workloads.

## Which GPU should you pick for DeepSeek-R1-0528?

GB300 on FP4, if you can get the cards. It's 14% faster than the B300 and 35% faster than the B200, with competitive latency (3,358.3ms TTFT, 33.6ms TPOT). If you're on AMD, the MI355X on FP4 with mori-sglang is the only option that cracks 4,000 tok/s/GPU. For last-gen hardware, the H200 on FP8 is the best you'll do at 2,453.9 tok/s/GPU, but you're leaving 7x throughput on the table vs. the GB300.

## FP4 vs FP8 on DeepSeek-R1-0528

On the GB300, FP4 nearly doubles FP8 throughput: 18,131.6 vs 8,980.3 tok/s/GPU. The gap holds across Blackwell cards. B200 on FP4 hits 13,429.7 tok/s/GPU while FP8 only manages 5,856.9. We haven't measured accuracy loss from FP4 on this model, so check your eval suite before committing. On AMD and Hopper hardware, only FP8 is available.

## dynamo-trt vs dynamo-sglang vs sglang

dynamo-trt wins on every NVIDIA Blackwell GPU by a wide margin. On the GB300, dynamo-trt gets 18,131.6 tok/s/GPU while dynamo-sglang manages 8,579.1 and plain sglang isn't tested. On AMD, mori-sglang is the top option at 4,222.8 tok/s/GPU, with atom at 3,652.0 and vanilla sglang at 1,952.2. Framework choice matters almost as much as the GPU here.

## Does disaggregated prefill help?

Yes, on NVIDIA Blackwell hardware. The GB300's best disagg config (18,131.6 tok/s/GPU) outperforms the best non-disagg GB200 result (11,585.2 tok/s/GPU) by 57%. On the B200, disagg pushes throughput from 10,505.4 to 13,429.7 tok/s/GPU, a 28% gain. Don't assume it helps everywhere, though. AMD configs don't show a disagg option in this data, and the overhead may not pay off at low concurrency.

## NVIDIA GB300 vs NVIDIA B300 on DeepSeek-R1-0528

GB300 wins on throughput: 18,131.6 vs 15,868.1 tok/s/GPU, a 14% gap. TPOT is close (33.6ms vs 38.5ms), and TTFT is similar too (3,358.3ms vs 2,773.4ms). The B300 actually has slightly lower TTFT. For batch throughput, GB300 is the pick. If your workload is latency-sensitive, the B300 is competitive enough that the cost difference might tip the decision.

## Fastest time to first token

AMD MI325X on FP8 with sglang at concurrency 64: 489.8ms TTFT. That's far faster than the GB300's 3,358.3ms, though throughput drops to 907.6 tok/s/GPU. The MI300X is close behind at 661.5ms. If TTFT matters more than throughput (interactive chat, for example), AMD's older GPUs at low concurrency have a real edge.
