DeepSeek-R1-0528 is a 671B mixture-of-experts reasoning model. Its size means most GPUs need multi-node setups with wide expert parallelism, and the choice of precision, framework, and disaggregated serving matters more than usual. We benchmarked it across 9 GPUs on [InferenceX](https://inferencex.semianalysis.com); for a smaller dense model comparison, see our [Llama-3.3-70B benchmarks](/blog/best-gpu-for-llama70b-inference).

## Key Findings

- NVIDIA GB300 on FP4 with dynamo-trt hits **18,131.6 tok/s/GPU**, the fastest config we tested for this model.
- NVIDIA B300 on FP4 comes in at 15,868.1 tok/s/GPU, only 12% behind the GB300 and ahead of the GB200.
- Disaggregated prefill with dynamo-trt dominates the top of the leaderboard. Every config above 8,000 tok/s/GPU uses disagg.
- AMD MI355X on FP4 with mori-sglang reaches 4,222.8 tok/s/GPU. That's 23% of the GB300, but it's the best AMD result by a wide margin.
- NVIDIA H100 still works at 850.1 tok/s/GPU on FP8, though you're leaving 95% of the GB300's throughput on the table.

## Which GPU should you pick for DeepSeek-R1-0528?

NVIDIA GB300 on FP4 with dynamo-trt, if you can get the hardware. It's 14% faster than B300 and nearly 4.3x faster than the best AMD option. For NVIDIA Blackwell without the NVL72 rack, B200 on FP4 still delivers 13,429.7 tok/s/GPU. If you're on AMD, MI355X on FP4 with mori-sglang is the only config that breaks 4,000 tok/s/GPU, though TTFT is high at 71,288.6ms due to the large concurrency and expert parallelism overhead.

## FP4 vs FP8 on DeepSeek-R1-0528

FP4 is the clear winner on every NVIDIA GPU where both are available. On GB300, FP4 hits 18,131.6 tok/s/GPU vs 8,980.3 for FP8, a 2x throughput gain. On B200, the gap is similar: 13,429.7 vs 5,856.9 tok/s/GPU. TPOT stays comparable between precisions (33.6ms vs 52.9ms on GB300). We haven't measured accuracy loss from FP4 quantization on this model, so check your eval suite before committing.

## dynamo-trt vs dynamo-sglang vs sglang

dynamo-trt wins on NVIDIA hardware across the board. On GB300, dynamo-trt at FP4 hits 18,131.6 tok/s/GPU while dynamo-sglang manages 7,120.5 tok/s/GPU at the same precision. On AMD, mori-sglang is the only disagg-capable framework and gets 4,222.8 tok/s/GPU on MI355X. Plain sglang without disagg tops out at 1,952.2 tok/s/GPU on MI355X. Framework choice on NVIDIA hardware is a 2.5x throughput swing.

## Does disaggregated prefill help?

Yes, on NVIDIA hardware it's dramatic. On GB200 with FP4, disagg pushes throughput from 11,585.2 to 14,952.0 tok/s/GPU (a 29% gain). On B200 with FP8, disagg gives 5,856.9 vs 3,467.2 tok/s/GPU without it (69% gain). TTFT does increase with disagg at high concurrency, but throughput scales much better. On AMD MI355X, disagg with mori-sglang (4,222.8 tok/s/GPU) beats non-disagg atom (3,652.0 tok/s/GPU) by 16%.

## NVIDIA GB300 vs NVIDIA B300 on DeepSeek-R1-0528

GB300 leads on throughput: 18,131.6 vs 15,868.1 tok/s/GPU, a 14% gap. TTFT is similar (3,358.3ms vs 2,773.4ms). B300 actually has a slightly lower TPOT of 38.5ms vs 33.6ms, but the difference is small. Both use FP4 with dynamo-trt and disagg. If you're choosing between these two for a DeepSeek-R1 deployment, GB300 is the better pick, but B300 gets you 88% of the throughput.

## Fastest time to first token

AMD MI325X on FP8 with sglang at concurrency 64: 489.8ms TTFT. That's far lower than anything on the NVIDIA disagg configs (the GB300's best TTFT is 2,639.5ms on FP8). Throughput drops to 907.6 tok/s/GPU at that concurrency, so this is only worth it if you're optimizing for interactive latency at low batch sizes. AMD MI300X is similar at 661.5ms TTFT.
