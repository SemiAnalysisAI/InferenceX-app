## Method

[AgentX](/agentx/methodology) replays real Claude Code sessions as deterministic [AIPerf](/glossary/aiperf) workloads. AgentX v1.0 has 393 published sessions, the median request in the full v1.0 corpus has 142,016 input tokens and 444 output tokens, and each run is profiled for 1 hour after a seeded warmup and prefix primers. The x-axis of every chart is p90 interactivity, 1 / p90 inter-token latency, in tok/s/user. The y-axis is the dashboard's default metric, total tokens per $1 TCO: total token throughput, which counts input tokens (including KV-cache hits) and output tokens, divided by the hourly cost of the GPUs serving it. Input is ~99% of that total.

Per-dollar figures use the hyperscaler owning cost from the [SemiAnalysis AI Cloud TCO Model](https://semianalysis.com/ai-cloud-tco-model/): $3.61/GPU/hr for Vera Rubin NVL72 and $2.31/GPU/hr for GB300 NVL72. InferenceX's [external and internal TCO bases](/glossary/internal-vs-external-tco) differ only for TPU v7, so both systems cost the same on either basis and every per-dollar ratio in this article holds on both. Per-MW figures divide throughput by the all-in power provisioned per GPU in the [SemiAnalysis Datacenter Industry Model](https://semianalysis.com/datacenter-industry-model/): 3.3 kW for Vera Rubin NVL72 and 2.12 kW for GB300 NVL72. These are planning figures for a deployed rack, including CPUs, networking and cooling. They are not measured draw during these runs. The cost ratio (1.563) is 0.4% above the power ratio (1.557), so every per-MW ratio between Vera Rubin and GB300 NVL72 in this article is 0.4% higher than the per-dollar ratio.

Matched-interactivity values are interpolated along each system's Pareto frontier, with no extrapolation past a curve's measured ends. GLM-5.2 is compared at 120 tok/s/user because its Vera Rubin curve starts at 111.5 tok/s/user. At each target the comparison uses the strongest GB300 NVL72 line at that point, whichever engine that is. Per dollar is the primary metric in the sections below, with per MW and per GPU as table columns.

## Specs

Vera Rubin NVL72 keeps the 72-GPU NVLink domain and MGX rack design of GB300 NVL72. Each Rubin GPU has the same 288 GB of HBM capacity and moves to HBM4 and NVLink 6.

| Per GPU                            | Rubin (Vera Rubin NVL72) | Blackwell Ultra (GB300 NVL72) | Rubin / BU |
| ---------------------------------- | -----------------------: | ----------------------------: | ---------: |
| HBM capacity                       |              288 GB HBM4 |  288 GB HBM3E (278 GB usable) |      1.00x |
| HBM bandwidth                      |                19.2 TB/s |                        8 TB/s |      2.40x |
| Dense NVFP4                        |               35 PFLOP/s |                    15 PFLOP/s |      2.33x |
| Dense FP8                          |             17.5 PFLOP/s |                     5 PFLOP/s |      3.50x |
| Dense BF16                         |                4 PFLOP/s |                   2.5 PFLOP/s |      1.60x |
| NVLink per GPU (uni-di)            |      1.5 TB/s (NVLink 6) |           900 GB/s (NVLink 5) |      1.67x |
| Scale-up world size                |                       72 |                            72 |      1.00x |
| TCO, owning at hyperscaler volume  |             $3.61/GPU/hr |                  $2.31/GPU/hr |      1.56x |
| All-in power per GPU (provisioned) |                   3.3 kW |                       2.12 kW |      1.56x |

Rubin values come from the Rubin GPU column of NVIDIA's [Vera Rubin NVL72 specifications](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/vera-rubin-nvl72/), which also lists 50 PFLOP/s per GPU of NVFP4 inference compute, footnoted as a sparse specification. NVIDIA's [Rubin](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/technologies/rubin/) page says Vera Rubin NVL72 has a new Transformer Engine with adaptive compression to boost NVFP4 inference performance. NVIDIA quotes [NVLink 6 at 3 TB/s per GPU](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/nvlink/), counting both directions, and 1.7x NVLink 5's 1.8 TB/s on the same basis. Blackwell Ultra values come from the [InferenceX GPU specs](/gpu-specs). TCO and power are the figures the dashboard uses.

Divided by the 1.56x cost ratio, the silicon ratios give Rubin 1.54x per dollar on HBM bandwidth, 1.49x on dense NVFP4 compute and 2.24x on dense FP8 compute. The per-dollar leads in the Summary table are above the HBM-bandwidth and NVFP4 figures and below the FP8 figure.

## DeepSeek-V4-Pro

Our [previous Vera Rubin article](/blog/vera-rubin-nvl72-agentic-inference) reported up to 7x better token throughput per megawatt for Vera Rubin NVL72 on DeepSeek-V4-Pro with pre-release software, including ~7.2x over GB300 NVL72 SGLang at 150 tok/s/user and 2.09x over the best GB300 NVL72 line at 100 tok/s/user. Both systems have newer runs since. The per-MW lead is now 6.92x at 150 tok/s/user. At 100 tok/s/user it fell from 2.09x to 1.90x, because GB300 NVL72's best line there rose from 28.5M to 44.5M tok/s/MW and Vera Rubin's from 59.4M to 84.6M.

On DeepSeek-V4-Pro the largest lead over GB300 NVL72 is ~8.3x at ~183 tok/s/user, per dollar and per MW. Above 137 tok/s/user every GB300 NVL72 frontier point serves 4 agents or fewer. At ~183 tok/s/user the strongest GB300 NVL72 line is Dynamo SGLang, whose value is interpolated between a 120-agent disaggregated config at 137 tok/s/user and a 4-agent config at 241 tok/s/user.

DeepSeek-V4-Pro has 1.6T total and 49B active parameters, with a hybrid of compressed sparse attention and heavily compressed attention that keeps its KV cache small at long context ([model card](https://huggingface.co/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4-Pro); see the [model page](/model/deepseek-v4)). Vera Rubin runs Dynamo TensorRT-LLM with FP4 weights and MTP, with prefill/decode disaggregation on 26 to 56 GPUs per configuration.

## Some caveats

First, engines differ and some runs are older. Vera Rubin runs Dynamo TRT-LLM on DeepSeek-V4-Pro and GLM-5.2, Dynamo vLLM on MiniMax-M3 and Dynamo SGLang on Qwen3.8-Flash-Next. The best GB300 NVL72 line at a given point can be a different engine, and some GB300 lines use a draft model for speculative decoding where Vera Rubin uses MTP. The GB300 NVL72 MiniMax-M3 results come from an older run. Second, Vera Rubin software is early, and the dashboard labels the Vera Rubin results an official preview. The images are internal TensorRT-LLM and vLLM builds and an SGLang nightly, and Vera Rubin's DeepSeek-V4-Pro curve moved 43% at 100 tok/s/user in under four weeks. Third, cost and power are modeled. The per-dollar numbers use modeled TCO per GPU-hour and the per-MW numbers use planning figures for all-in power per GPU. Neither comes from billing or telemetry for these runs. A different Vera Rubin cost or power figure would scale every Vera Rubin per-dollar or per-MW ratio in this article by the same factor. Fourth, some ratios span coverage gaps. Where a curve has no measured config across a wide span of interactivity, values inside that span are interpolated, and a ratio there measures the gap as well as the hardware. This is the main reason for the 6.9x to ~8.3x figures on DeepSeek-V4-Pro from 150 to 200 tok/s/user. Vera Rubin's own curves have gaps on DeepSeek-V4-Pro (97 to 165 and 230 to 310 tok/s/user), MiniMax-M3 (128 to 244 and 244 to 413 tok/s/user), Qwen3.8-Flash-Next (24 to 130 and 173 to 271 tok/s/user) and GLM-5.2 (111 to 262 tok/s/user).
